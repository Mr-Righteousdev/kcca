# Website Observation Report — Kampala Capital City Authority (KCCA)
**URL:** https://www.kcca.go.ug
**Course Unit:** User Interface Design

---

## 1. Introduction

This report presents the findings of a UI observation conducted on the Kampala Capital City Authority website. KCCA is the statutory body established by the Ugandan Parliament to manage and operate the capital city of Kampala. Its website serves as the primary digital gateway for residents, businesses, developers, and government partners seeking to interact with the authority — from applying for trading licences and development permits, to accessing employment opportunities, tracking tenders, and submitting citizen feedback.

The evaluation assessed how well the site serves the everyday needs of a Kampala resident or business owner who has come to the site to get something done. It examined navigation clarity, visual design quality, mobile usability, the accessibility of key services, and the degree to which the site makes KCCA feel approachable and functional rather than bureaucratic.

---

## 2. Background of the Website

KCCA was established in 2011 under the Kampala Capital City Authority Act, replacing the former Kampala City Council. It operates across eleven directorates covering areas from physical planning, revenue collection, and engineering to public health, education, and gender and community services. The website at kcca.go.ug is the authority's main public-facing digital platform, hosted under the `.go.ug` government domain.

The site currently lists a toll-free line (0800 199 000), a general email address (info@kcca.go.ug), and links to several external portals for specific services — including the eCitie payment system (ecitie2.kcca.go.ug), the Smart Permit portal for development applications (kcca.iras.go.ug), a document tracking portal (camcamv.kcca.go.ug), and the Visit Kampala tourism site (visitkampala.kcca.go.ug).

---

## 3. Objectives of the Evaluation

The evaluation was structured around the following questions:

- Can a Kampala resident quickly find the service they are looking for?
- Does the visual design reflect the authority of a capital city government?
- Does the homepage communicate what KCCA does and how citizens can engage with it?
- Does the site work correctly and clearly on a mobile device?
- Are key citizen-facing services — licences, permits, feedback, jobs — easy to access without confusion?

---

## 4. Methodology

The observer navigated the homepage, the FAQs page, the Contact Us page, the Citizen Feedback page, the Services section, the Tenders listing, the Careers section, and several Directorate sub-pages. The site was tested at mobile screen width to assess responsiveness. Navigation dropdowns were expanded and tested. The relationship between the main site and its multiple external portal links was noted and assessed from the perspective of a first-time visitor.

---

## 5. Strengths

### 5.1  Toll-Free Citizen Helpline is Prominently Displayed

The toll-free contact number (0800 199 000) appears in the site header on every page, making it immediately visible to any visitor regardless of where they land. For a government authority serving a population that includes citizens with varying levels of digital literacy, having a free phone option visible at all times is a sound and important design decision.

### 5.2  Comprehensive Coverage of Directorates and Services

The site provides dedicated pages for all eleven KCCA directorates — from Physical Planning and Treasury Services to Gender and Community Services and Internal Audit. Every major service area the authority operates is represented online, giving residents and business operators a single platform to understand the full scope of KCCA's work.

### 5.3  Active News Section with Timely Updates

The homepage features a news section that is genuinely up to date, carrying recent stories including coverage of the new Lord Mayor's inauguration in May 2026. This active news presence gives the site credibility as a live channel of communication between the authority and the public, rather than a static brochure.

### 5.4  Social Media Integration and Multi-Platform Presence

Links to KCCA's official pages on Facebook, Twitter, YouTube, Instagram, and Flickr are present in the site header. This multi-platform presence supports broader digital engagement and gives citizens additional channels to follow city news, announcements, and project updates.

### 5.5  Citizen Feedback Mechanism is Present

The site includes a dedicated Citizen Feedback page, giving residents a structured way to submit feedback directly to the authority online. The availability of this channel — however basic in its current form — reflects a commitment to two-way communication between KCCA and the residents of Kampala.

### 5.6  Multiple Online Service Portals are Linked

The homepage provides links to several functional online portals — including eCitie for payments, Smart Permit for development applications, and a document tracking system. The intent to provide citizens with digital access to core KCCA transactions is clearly present.

---

## 6. Weaknesses

### 6.1  Navigation is Overloaded and Difficult to Use

The main navigation bar contains a large number of items, with dropdown menus that expand into long, ungrouped lists of links. The Directorates menu alone contains eleven items with no sub-grouping by function or audience. A resident who has come to the site to find out how to apply for a trading licence, report an illegal construction, or apply for a job must read through multiple long dropdown lists before finding their destination. There is no top-level separation between citizen-facing services and internal/institutional information, meaning a business owner and an infrastructure engineer are presented with the same undifferentiated menu.

> **Impact:** An overloaded navigation makes the most commonly needed services — licences, permits, jobs, feedback — no faster to reach than the least-used institutional pages. Citizens who do not already know how KCCA is structured cannot navigate by department names they have never heard of.

---

### 6.2  The Homepage Does Not Communicate What a New Visitor Should Do

The homepage does not have a clear call-to-action or a citizen journey guide. A first-time visitor — a business owner wanting a trading licence, a developer needing a building permit, or a resident wanting to report a problem — lands on a page that shows a news carousel, a row of portal tiles, and a navigation bar, but receives no prompt or guidance on where to start. The site assumes the visitor already knows what they need and where to look for it. Citizens who do not come with that prior knowledge are left to self-navigate without help.

> **Impact:** A significant proportion of visitors to a government website arrive with a specific task but without knowledge of the institution's structure. Without guided pathways, these visitors are likely to call the helpline or visit offices in person for tasks they could complete online.

---

### 6.3  External Portals are Confusing and Fragmented

Several of KCCA's key citizen services — online payments, permit applications, plan approval tracking, and document tracking — are hosted on entirely separate subdomains with different interfaces, different branding, and different login systems. A citizen clicking through from the main site to ecitie2.kcca.go.ug, kcca.iras.go.ug, and camcamv.kcca.go.ug will encounter three visually distinct platforms that feel unconnected from each other and from the main site. One of these sub-portals (the document tracking system at camcamv.kcca.go.ug) still carries a footer that recommends Internet Explorer 9 — a browser discontinued in 2016.

> **Impact:** Fragmented portals with inconsistent branding reduce citizen confidence and make it unclear whether they are on an official government platform. The Internet Explorer recommendation on the document tracking portal is a visible signal of outdated infrastructure.

---

### 6.4  The Site Has a Dated Visual Design

The overall visual design of the site does not reflect the standing of the authority responsible for governing Uganda's capital city. The homepage layout is dense and flat, with limited use of visual hierarchy to help the eye find important content. Typography is basic, imagery is inconsistently treated, and the overall colour application lacks the confidence and clarity expected of a modern government digital presence. Comparable capital city authorities across Africa — including Nairobi City County and the City of Cape Town — present significantly more polished and citizen-centric web experiences.

> **Impact:** Visual design on a government website functions as a trust signal. A site that looks underfunded or outdated can reduce citizens' confidence in the competency of the authority it represents, and may deter engagement — particularly among younger, more digitally active residents.

---

### 6.5  Mobile Experience is Weak

At mobile screen widths, the site's navigation does not collapse cleanly into a usable hamburger menu. Dropdown menus are difficult to open and navigate on a touchscreen. Some page content overflows horizontally. Given that a majority of Kampala residents access the internet primarily on mobile devices, a navigation and layout structure that works best on a desktop monitor leaves a large proportion of the city's population with a degraded experience on the site of the body that governs them.

> **Impact:** Poor mobile usability on a government platform is not merely a design problem — it is an access and equity issue. Services that are available in principle but difficult to reach in practice on the devices most citizens use are effectively less available.

---

### 6.6  The FAQ Page Covers Only Physical Planning

The site has a single FAQ page, and it covers only development applications under the Physical Planning Directorate. There are no FAQs for the services citizens ask about most frequently — trading licences, business registration, garbage collection, road repairs, employment applications, or school enrolment at KCCA schools. A resident searching the site for guidance on how to apply for a licence or report a service failure finds no self-service answers available, and is left with a phone number and an email address as their only options.

> **Impact:** A FAQ page limited to one niche technical area does not serve the majority of citizens visiting the site. The absence of answers to common questions pushes avoidable contact volume to the helpline and frontline offices.

---

## 7. Conclusion

The KCCA website covers the breadth of the authority's work and provides genuine access to several online services through its linked portals. The presence of a toll-free number in the header, an active news section, and a citizen feedback channel demonstrate a meaningful intent to serve the public digitally.

However, the site is significantly held back by an overloaded navigation structure, the absence of guided citizen pathways on the homepage, a fragmented multi-portal architecture with inconsistent branding, a dated visual design, and a weak mobile experience. For a government authority with a mandate to serve the 1.5 million residents of Kampala — the majority of whom primarily access the internet on mobile phones — these are not cosmetic problems. They are barriers between citizens and the services they are entitled to access.

The redesign should prioritise: a citizen-first navigation model organised by task rather than by directorate, a homepage that actively guides different user types toward their destination, a consolidated and consistently branded service experience, improved mobile responsiveness, and a comprehensive FAQ that covers the questions KCCA's frontline staff answer every day.
